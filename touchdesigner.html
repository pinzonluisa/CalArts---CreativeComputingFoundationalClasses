Intro to TouchDesigner

This course teaches TouchDesigner, a node-based program for building real-time visuals. Students build a new network every week, starting with a simple color palette and working up to audio-reactive and 3D work. The semester ends with a live group performance.

Credit Load

Credit Load

This is a 3-credit course, following CalArts’ standard credit-hour policy: 45 hours of work per credit for BFA students, 60 hours per credit for Masters students. That comes to 135 total hours for BFA students and 180 for Masters students across the semester. In-class time adds up to about 25.7 hours over the semester. The rest is independent work: roughly 7.8 hours a week for BFA students, and roughly 11 hours a week for Masters students.

Overview

Course Description

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to TouchDesigner, a leading platform for interactive visual design. Students explore audio reactivity and dynamic visuals, introduction to 3D modeling and rendering, essential TouchDesigner components and workflows, and building interactive, real-time visual systems. No prior TouchDesigner experience is necessary.

A typical 1h50m session opens with a few minutes for questions, then moves straight into building that week’s network for the rest of class. There usually isn’t time for a separate lecture or homework preview; that’s part of why every session is recorded.

Learning outcomes

  • Develop technical proficiency across music technology tools and workflows. Real-time audio-visual environments, matching audio-reactive visuals and TouchDesigner’s own workflow.
  • Create immersive virtual and augmented environments. The 3D introduction and the Instancing/POPs work that follows.
  • Integrate spatial media into cohesive experiential systems. Building interactive, real-time visual systems across the semester, especially the Week 11–13 cross-tool final project work.

Masters students additionally build toward leading interdisciplinary teams on the required group final, independent research beyond what’s shown in class, and articulating the thinking behind spatial and visual work.

Final Project

Final Project

This is a live group performance, in person, on Wednesday, December 2, within the 1–6 PM window. This matches Coding Music Fundamentals and Coding Art Fundamentals exactly. Solo projects are not allowed.

Group size
Minimum 2 people, maximum 3.
Forming your group
Around Week 4, once rosters are settled, a chart goes out where each student checks off their own background (musician, visual artist, prior coding experience) plus notes, so students who don’t know each other can find a match. Week 9 is the deadline to have a group formed. Unformed groups get assigned that day, and members exchange contact information.
TouchDesigner requirement
Every TouchDesigner-enrolled student in a group contributes at least one properly built, genuinely real-time network: audio-reactive, OSC/MIDI-driven, or generative, not a pre-rendered video played back with nothing live behind it.
Masters student leadership
Each group should include at least one Masters student, when enrollment allows. Masters students are expected to take a leading role in their group. It’s a chance to practice the leadership and professionalism needed on mixed-experience teams, in school and afterward.
Connecting outward
Every group’s TouchDesigner content needs to connect to at least one other piece of software or language, such as ChucK, p5.js, openFrameworks, or Ableton, or anything that speaks OSC, MIDI, or another live protocol. You aren’t limited to this list; any tool that fits the piece works.
Reusing one project
If a group’s project counts as the final for more than one of Luisa’s three Fall 2026 courses, it has to incorporate each tool it’s counting for: two tools if double-counted, all three if triple-counted.
Digital submission
Week 14 (Dec 9): a high-quality video/audio recording, due the same day, for grading and the Digital Expo portfolio. Specs go out by Week 12. It doesn’t have to be a straight capture of the live show. This matches Coding Music Fundamentals and Coding Art Fundamentals exactly; there are no exceptions for any of the three courses.
